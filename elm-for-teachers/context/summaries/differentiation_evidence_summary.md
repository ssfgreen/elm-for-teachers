# Differentiation and Adaptive Teaching: Evidence Summary (data context)

**Type:** Reference document for agentic systems. Condensed from the full research report (ELM for Teachers, 15 October 2026). Read for any planning, differentiation, grouping or SEND/ASN/ALN task.
**Version:** 0.1, 16 October 2026.

## 1. The headline

Teach the same ambitious content to the whole class, with planned scaffolds that fade, explicit instruction, access adaptations and temporary mastery-based groups. This is what the DfE calls **adaptive teaching** and what the EEF calls high-quality teaching for pupils with SEND. "Differentiation by task" that gives some pupils different, easier content has weak evidence and risks narrowing the curriculum for those pupils.

## 2. Strength key

- **Strong:** consistent rigorous evidence (RCTs, meta-analyses, practice guides with strong ratings).
- **Moderate:** some rigorous studies or meta-analyses with heterogeneity.
- **Emerging:** promising but limited.
- **Consensus:** professional or expert guidance with little causal evidence.
- **Negative:** evidence of harm or no effect.

## 3. Cross-phase evidence

| Approach | Do | Strength | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explicit instruction | Model, worked examples, guided then independent practice, non-examples | Strong | EEF SEND; IES maths guide 2021 |
| Scaffolding that fades | Frames, starters, faded examples; remove as skill grows | Strong to moderate | EEF SEND; worked-example research |
| Cognitive and metacognitive strategies | Teach planning, monitoring, evaluating | Strong | EEF SEND; EEF Metacognition |
| Flexible grouping | Temporary, by current mastery | Moderate | EEF SEND; EEF Toolkit within-class grouping (+3 months, earlier analysis) |
| Technology | Model, practise, give access; adaptive software | Moderate | Deunk et al. 2018; EEF SEND |
| Universal Design for Learning | Multiple means of engagement, representation, expression | Moderate to emerging | King-Sears et al. 2023 (g = 0.43, 20 studies, inconsistent implementation) |
| Setting and streaming | Avoid as default | Negative for low attainers | EEF Toolkit (0 months average; worse for low attainers; contested detail) |
| Learning-styles matching | Do not use | Negative | Pashler et al. 2008 |
| Differentiated instruction overall | Positive, modest, fragile | Small (primary); d = +0.51 to +0.74 (secondary, 14 studies) | Deunk et al. 2018; Smale-Jacobse et al. 2019 |

## 4. Phase notes

- **Primary:** Effects are small and come mainly from computerised systems or whole-programme reforms. Low-SES pupils risk early low-ability labelling. Pre-teaching Tier 2 vocabulary is a KS2 literacy recommendation. ADHD interventions are more effective in primary than secondary (Yegencik, Bell and Deniz 2025).
- **Secondary:** Thin but positive evidence; teachers in studies rarely implemented elaborate differentiation. Disciplinary literacy (explicit vocabulary, morphology, graphic organisers) is the secondary literacy recommendation. DLD persists into secondary; the Word Discovery approach shows gains in definitions. High-attainer differentiation evidence is inconclusive (Ziernwald et al. 2022).

## 5. Need groups: one line each (see individual profiles for detail)

| Group | Strongest evidenced response | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Dyslexia / literacy difficulties | Structured literacy and phonics; BDA formatting for access | Strong (instruction); consensus (format) |
| Maths difficulties | Systematic instruction, representations, number line, precise language, word-problem schemas | Strong (IES 2021, all six recommendations strong) |
| Autism | Visual supports, task analysis, prompting, video modelling, peer-based instruction | Strong (single-case dominated; contested) |
| ADHD | Academic interventions, self-monitoring, daily report cards, chunking with feedback | Moderate (DuPaul et al. 2012) |
| SLCN / DLD | Pre-teach vocabulary with phonological and semantic features; reduce language load, keep concepts | Moderate to emerging |
| Hearing impairment | Seating, radio aids, captions, acoustics | Consensus / standards |
| Visual impairment | Modified large print rebuilt digitally; simplified diagrams; QTVI | Consensus / standards |
| SEMH | Predictable routines, explicit learning behaviours, relationships | Moderate (behaviour) |
| EAL | Explicit vocabulary, elaboration not simplification, oral rehearsal | Moderate |
| High attainers | Depth within the topic | Emerging / inconclusive |

## 6. Teaching assistants

More TA support was associated with less progress in the DISS project (separation from teacher and peers). Structured TA-led interventions add 3 to 4 months on average. TAs should not be the default teacher of the lowest attainers (EEF).

## 7. AI and differentiation

The EEF/NFER ChatGPT trial cut weekly planning time by 31% with no visible loss of quality (independent panel), and "tailoring existing materials to specific groups" was a common use. No pupil outcomes were measured. The EEF Aila trial adds that time saved does not mean better teaching; teachers must check and adapt outputs. No UK trial has yet tested AI-supported differentiation on attainment.

## 8. The invariant for agents

Same objective, same key terms, more routes in. Change the route, not the destination. Decisions that change the destination belong to teachers, SENCos / ASL leads and statutory processes.
