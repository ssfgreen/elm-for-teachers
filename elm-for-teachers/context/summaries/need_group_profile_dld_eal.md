# Need-Group Profile: Developmental Language Disorder (DLD) and Multilingual Learners (EAL) (data context)

**Type:** Reference document for agentic systems. One of a set of need-group profiles. Written as **barriers and evidenced responses**, not as a description of a pupil.
**Version:** 0.1, 16 October 2026.
**Why these two together:** Both groups are often mistaken for low attainers and both benefit from explicit vocabulary teaching. They differ in cause and in long-term trajectory, so the profile keeps them distinct within each section.

## 1. Scope and prevalence

- **DLD:** Norbury et al. (2016, SCALES cohort, Surrey): prevalence 7.58% at school entry, roughly two children in every class of 30. DLD is persistent into secondary school and adulthood. Terminology from the CATALISE consensus (Bishop et al., 2017): "DLD" replaces "specific language impairment".
- **EAL:** A language status, not a need. Over a fifth of pupils in England have a first language other than English (DfE school census; verify current figure). Proficiency in English ranges from new to English to fluent. Many EAL pupils outperform peers once proficient.
- **Overlap:** A multilingual pupil can also have DLD; DLD is diagnosed in the pupil's strongest language and should never be inferred from English proficiency alone.

## 3. Evidenced responses (what to do), with strength

| Response | What it looks like | Strength | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-teach key vocabulary | 5 to 10 words, adult-led, before whole-class teaching; retrieval later | Moderate (EAL); emerging to moderate (DLD) | EEF KS2 literacy; Oxley and de Cat 2019; Lowe et al. 2018 |
| Teach phonological and semantic features of words | Syllables, first sound, rhyme; category, function, word map | Emerging to moderate (DLD) | Lowe, Henry and Joffe 2019 (Word Discovery, Year 7 science) |
| Reduce language load of instructions, keep concepts | One instruction per line; short sentences; visuals; comprehension check | Consensus (DLD) | RCSLT guidance; EEF SEND |
| Elaborate rather than simplify texts | Glosses in brackets, explicit connectives, redundancy; original wording retained | Moderate (EAL) | Yano, Long and Ross 1994 |
| Visual supports and dual coding | Diagrams paired with words; graphic organisers | Moderate (general) | EEF secondary literacy; cognitive load research |
| Oral rehearsal before writing | Say it before you write it; sentence stems; talk partners | Moderate | EEF Oral Language Interventions (+6 months average, Toolkit) |
| Processing time | Wait time after questions; no cold-calling without warning | Consensus | RCSLT; EEF SEND |
| Home-language use as a bridge | Bilingual glossaries; peers; allow planning in home language | Emerging | Bell Foundation; Murphy and Unthiah 2015 |
| Plan for persistence (DLD) | Vocabulary support continues through secondary; DLD does not resolve by itself | Strong (epidemiology) | Norbury et al. 2016 |

## 4. What not to do

- Do not treat EAL as low attainment or assign EAL pupils to the "easy" task.
- Do not infer DLD from an EAL pupil's English.
- Do not strip academic vocabulary from texts; replace exposure with support.
- Do not expect DLD to "catch up" by secondary; keep supports in place.
- Do not ask the agent to diagnose. Referral is for the SENCo / ASL lead and speech and language therapy.
