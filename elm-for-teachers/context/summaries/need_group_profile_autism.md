# Need-Group Profile: Autistic Pupils (data context)

**Type:** Reference document for agentic systems. Written as barriers and evidenced responses. Autism is heterogeneous; this profile lists common barriers, not a description of any pupil.
**Version:** 0.1, 16 October 2026.

## 1. Scope

Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference affecting social communication, sensory processing and preference for predictability. Around 1 to 2% of pupils have an autism diagnosis (verify current census figures by jurisdiction). Many autistic pupils are in mainstream classes without additional statutory plans. Language preference: ask the school; identity-first ("autistic pupil") is widely preferred by autistic communities.

## 2. Common barriers in the classroom

| Barrier | Example |
|---|---|
| Unpredictability and transitions | Distress at timetable changes, cover teachers, moving between activities |
| Implicit instructions and idiom | "Pull your socks up"; unstated expectations |
| Sensory load | Noise, lighting, crowded corridors, textures |
| Multi-step tasks with no visible structure | Starting and sequencing open tasks |
| Group work and social demand | Turn-taking, unstructured discussion |
| Anxiety | Masking, shutdown, school avoidance |
| Strengths often overlooked | Detail focus, memory for facts, deep interests |

## 3. Evidenced responses

| Response | What it looks like | Strength | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual supports | Visual schedules, now/next, labelled areas, visual boundaries | Strong (65 studies, NCAEP) | Steinbrenner et al. 2020 |
| Task analysis | Multi-step tasks broken into observable steps with a checklist | Strong | NCAEP |
| Prompting and fading | Structured prompts removed as independence grows | Strong | NCAEP |
| Antecedent-based changes | Change the environment or demand before the difficulty arises (seating, warnings, sensory breaks) | Strong | NCAEP |
| Video modelling | Short videos of the expected task or routine | Strong | NCAEP |
| Social narratives | Short written explanations of a situation and what to expect | Strong (as listed); contested in purpose | NCAEP; critiques |
| Peer-based instruction | Structured peer support | Strong | NCAEP |
| Technology-aided instruction | Apps for schedules, communication, practice | Strong | NCAEP |
| Explicit, literal instructions | Say exactly what is wanted; avoid idiom; one instruction at a time | Consensus | NAS; EEF SEND |
| Change warnings | Advance notice of changes, with a visual | Consensus | NAS |
| Sensory adjustments | Quiet space, ear defenders, seating choice | Consensus | NAS |
| Interest-based engagement | Link tasks to deep interests where possible | Emerging | Various |

Note on evidence: NCAEP's 28 evidence-based practices rest heavily on single-case designs; effect sizes are not comparable with group designs. Leaf et al. (2021) criticised the review's methods; neurodiversity-affirming critics question compliance-focused goals. Agents should present strategies as options with their stated purpose.

## 4. What not to do

- Do not build compliance or "normalising" goals into resources; predictability and access are the aims.
- Do not assume every autistic pupil needs the same supports; ask what works for this pupil.
- Do not use deficit language ("lacks empathy", "rigid").
- Do not invent a symbol set; use the school's.
- Do not replace a trusted adult with a resource.
