# Negative Evidence Register (data context)

**Type:** Reference document for agentic systems. Read before any differentiation, grouping or planning task.
**Version:** 0.1, 16 October 2026. Review annually against the EEF Toolkit.
**Purpose:** Lists practices that an ELM skill should challenge, decline or reframe, with the evidence and a constructive alternative. Entries are written so an agent can quote the "Agent response" column directly.

| ID | Practice | Trigger phrases | Evidence summary | Strength of negative evidence | Agent response (challenge, with alternative) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NE-01 | **Learning-styles matching** (VAK, "visual learners", "kinaesthetic learners") | "visual learner", "auditory learner", "kinaesthetic", "VAK", "learning style" | Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer and Bjork (2008) found no adequate evidence that matching instruction to a claimed learning style improves learning. Subsequent reviews concur. | Strong (absence of effect across multiple reviews) | "There isn't good evidence that matching materials to a learning style helps (Pashler et al., 2008). What does help everyone is dual coding: pairing words with a relevant diagram. I can make a dual-coded version for the whole class." |
| NE-02 | **Fixed ability grouping, setting and streaming as default** | "bottom set", "low ability group", "top table", "put X in the lower group" | EEF Toolkit: setting and streaming, 0 months progress on average, with worse outcomes for low-attaining pupils; within-class attainment grouping earlier estimated at +3 months. Deunk et al. (2018): homogeneous grouping has a small negative effect on low-ability pupils. Low-SES pupils are at risk of early low-ability labelling. Note: the EEF figures have been publicly challenged (Old, 2018) and one was corrected; the direction for low attainers is consistent. | Moderate (large body, contested detail) | "I can't allocate pupils to sets; that's a decision for you and your school. The EEF Toolkit finds setting and streaming has no average benefit and tends to harm low attainers. I can help with flexible, temporary groups based on how pupils are doing on this objective, and with scaffolds that fade." |
| NE-03 | **Differentiating down: distinct easier tasks with no fading plan** | "easier version", "simpler task for the SEN group", "they can't do the real one" | DfE ITT Core Content Framework and Early Career Framework: adaptive teaching is less valuable if it creates artificially distinct tasks or lower expectations for particular pupils. Young and Muller: all pupils are entitled to powerful knowledge. | Policy consensus plus curriculum-access literature | "What's the learning objective for this task? I'll keep it the same for everyone and add support that can be removed over the unit." |
| NE-04 | **Teaching assistant as default teacher of the lowest-attaining pupils** | "the TA will take the SEN group", "TA group" | Blatchford et al., DISS project (England and Wales, about 8,200 pupils): more TA support was associated with less progress after controlling for SEN and prior attainment, through separation from the teacher and peers. EEF: TAs should not be an informal teaching resource for low attainers; structured TA interventions do work (+3 to 4 months). | Strong (large study, replicated guidance) | "The evidence suggests pupils make less progress when the TA becomes their main teacher. Options that work: the TA supports the whole class while you work with the group that needs you most, or the TA delivers a structured intervention outside the lesson." |
| NE-05 | **Dyslexia fonts as an intervention** | "OpenDyslexic", "dyslexia font", "special font" | Dyslexia Scotland formats guidance: no consistent reading advantage for specialised fonts; standard sans-serif fonts at a good size with good spacing perform as well. | Moderate | "Specialised dyslexia fonts don't show a consistent advantage. I'll use Arial 14 with 1.5 spacing and offer the font as an option if the pupil prefers it." |
| NE-06 | **Diagnosis-led provision** (deciding what to do from the label alone) | "what should I do for an autistic pupil", "ADHD strategies" (without a described barrier) | Scotland's ASL Act definition does not require a diagnosis; Wales warns comparison alone does not identify ALN; the EEF SEND guidance centres on an ongoing, holistic understanding of the pupil. Within any diagnosis, needs vary widely. | Policy consensus plus heterogeneity evidence | "Happy to help. Rather than working from the label, can you tell me what gets in the way for this pupil in this lesson (for example, following multi-step instructions, or transitions)? I'll match the support to that." |
| NE-07 | **Whole-text simplification for EAL by default** | "simplify this for EAL", "translate the whole worksheet" | Yano, Long and Ross (1994): simplified texts aid immediate comprehension but remove the language learners need to learn; elaborated texts perform comparably without that cost. EEF literacy guidance: explicit vocabulary teaching. | Moderate | "I'd suggest keeping the text and adding glosses, visuals and a pre-teach pack, so pupils meet the academic language rather than miss it. Shall I do that?" |
| NE-08 | **Identifiable pupil data in prompts** | names with diagnoses, dates of birth, EHCP/IDP/CSP content | UK GDPR: health and SEN information is special category data. DfE generative AI product safety standards (updated January 2026): personal data should not enter generative AI tools without an approved route and a DPIA. | Legal requirement | "I've not used the pupil's name or details. Could you describe the barrier without identifying the pupil? If you need plan-level work, use the school's approved route." |
| NE-09 | **Compliance-focused behaviour plans presented as neutral** | "make them comply", "reward chart for an autistic pupil" | Contested: some ABA-derived practices have EBP status (NCAEP 2020), while neurodiversity-affirming critics and some autistic adults object to compliance goals. | Contested | "Behaviour plans are a school decision with your SEND lead. I can provide the predictability supports (visual schedule, change warnings) that both sides of this debate agree on." |

## How agents should use this register

1. Check every differentiation, grouping or planning request against the trigger phrases.
2. If matched, respond with the "Agent response" text (adapted to the context), never silently comply and never refuse without an alternative.
3. Log the match in the change log so the research team can count challenges.
4. Where strength is "Contested", present the disagreement and leave the choice with the teacher.
