# Pre-teach vocabulary (EAL and SLCN)

## What this skill does

Selects a small, central set of words from a topic and builds the materials a teacher or teaching assistant needs to pre-teach them: definitions, images, examples, oral rehearsal and a retrieval schedule. The whole-class text stays as it is.

## Invariants

1. Five to ten words, no more. If the teacher wants more, split into two packs.
2. Words are ranked by centrality to the learning objective, not by rarity.
3. Academic vocabulary is kept, never replaced. The pack helps pupils reach the words, not avoid them.
4. Home-language glosses are marked "to be verified" and never presented as authoritative.
5. EAL is not treated as a learning difficulty. Do not combine EAL with low-attainment assumptions.
6. No identifiable pupil data.

## Inputs

- The topic text, unit outline or word list.
- Learning objective.
- Phase and subject.
- Pupil languages, if the teacher wants glosses (optional).
- Whether the pack is for EAL, DLD or both (changes step 4).

## Steps

1. **Extract candidates.** List all words a pupil would need to understand to meet the objective. Mark each Tier 2 (general academic, e.g. "analyse", "structure") or Tier 3 (subject-specific, e.g. "photosynthesis").
2. **Rank and cut.** Keep the 5 to 10 words most central to the objective. Note why each was chosen in one line.
3. **Build a card per word:**
   - The word, with syllables marked and the first sound (helps DLD).
   - A pupil-friendly definition in plain English that still uses the correct concept.
   - An image or image suggestion.
   - One example sentence from the topic and one non-example.
   - Morphology where useful ("photo" = light, "synthesis" = putting together).
   - Home-language gloss, flagged "verify with a speaker or dictionary".
4. **Add oral rehearsal prompts:** two questions that make the pupil say the word in a sentence ("Which of these is an example of condensation, and why?"). For DLD, add a semantic map (category, function, related words) and a phonological cue, following the Word Discovery approach.
5. **Schedule retrieval:** day 1 pre-teach, day 2 recap, during the lesson, one week later. Include a two-minute retrieval quiz.
6. **Write a short script** for whoever delivers the pre-teach (teacher or TA), ten minutes, with the words in the order they appear in the lesson.
7. **Run the checks.**

## Checks

- [ ] 5 to 10 words.
- [ ] Each word is in the source material and central to the objective.
- [ ] Definitions do not introduce errors (flag any you are unsure of).
- [ ] Glosses flagged for verification.
- [ ] No simplification of the whole-class text.
- [ ] Retrieval schedule present.

## Output

1. Word cards (5 to 10).
2. Pre-teach script (10 minutes).
3. Retrieval schedule and quiz.
4. Change log and sources.

## When to refuse or redirect

- "Translate the whole worksheet": offer the pack and an elaborated version (glosses in brackets) instead; machine translation of whole resources is a teacher decision and should be checked by a speaker.
- "The EAL pupils need the easy version": explain that EAL is a language need, not an attainment level, and offer the pack.

## Sources

- EEF, Improving Literacy in Key Stage 2 (second edition): pre-teaching Tier 2 vocabulary through an adult before whole-class reading.
- EEF, Improving Literacy in Secondary Schools: disciplinary vocabulary, etymology and morphology, graphic organisers.
- DfE, The Reading Framework (2023): EAL pupils need more explicit vocabulary teaching, including visuals.
- Murphy and Unthiah (2015), Bell Foundation: systematic review of EAL interventions.
- Oxley and de Cat (2019), Language Learning Journal: classroom vocabulary interventions, larger gains for lowest pre-test scorers.
- Lowe, Henry and Joffe (2019): Word Discovery, phonological and semantic features for secondary pupils with (D)LD.
- Yano, Long and Ross (1994): elaboration keeps the linguistic items learners need to learn; simplification removes them.
