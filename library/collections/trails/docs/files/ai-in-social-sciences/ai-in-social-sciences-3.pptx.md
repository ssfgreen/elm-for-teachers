## Slide 4

Write down three observations from this table.

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Introduce and explain The AI Assessment Scale. Clearly describe each of the five levels (NO AI, AI PLANNING, AI COLLABORATION, FULL AI, AI EXPLORATION) and what each level entails in terms of student and AI involvement. Pose the question: "Is using AI cheating? Create three arguments for and against this and use the scale to help". Facilitate a discussion using the assessment scale as a framework for understanding different levels of AI integration and their potential implications for academic integrity. The starter task included examples of inaccuracies where AI was used. What problems could this lead to? Is the purpose of the assessment scale only to stop cheating and protect academic integrity or is it also to ensure information is checked? Reference for the AI Assessment Scale: Perkins, M., Furze, L. Roe, J. & MacVaugh, J. (2024). The Artificial Intelligence Assessment Scale (AIAS): A framework for ethical integration of Generative AI in Education Assessment. Journal of University Teaching and Learning Practice, 21(6), 49-66. https://search.informit.org/doi/10.3316/informit.T2024092900003300954126858

## Slide 8

TRAILS.scot

Is AI more or less reliable for insight than humans? Discuss this in your row.

Consider:

Experience. Can AI experience events firsthand?

Knowledge. Can humans know everything?

Creativity. What/who is better at thinking of solutions?

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Instruct students to choose a real-world issue (e.g., social inequality, clean energy, migration patterns, justice in society). Guide them on using an AI platform to create a presentation outline on their chosen issue. Emphasize the importance of creating a good prompt, highlighting the elements of a good prompt provided on the slide: Persona: Ask the tool to take a role (e.g., a helpful tutor). Objective: What do you want the AI to do? Audience: Specify who it's for. Context: What does the tool need to know? Boundaries: Set your own direction & limitation (e.g., length, tone). You may want to model creating a prompt with the class. Suggested AI tools include: MagicSchools AI, ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Brisk Source for "Elements of a Good Prompt": Five quick prompting tips to get more from your AI assistant | Microsoft Education Blog

## Slide 9

Choose a real world issue and using a AI platform we will create a presentation outline on this.

Consider issues such as social inequality, clean energy, migration patterns or justice in society.

When creating your presentation outline be very careful with your prompt.

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Set a time limit (e.g., five minutes) for students to take their AI-generated presentation outline and begin turning it into a presentation. Encourage students to consider what they need to add to the AI-generated content to make it suitable for their intended audience and purpose. Prompt them to think about whether they can use AI to speed up this process (e.g., generating images, finding sources). ## Slide 10

TRAILS.scot

Task: Create the Presentation

You have only five minutes to take your AI generated presentation outline and turn it into a presentation!

Consider what you need to add and what matters. Can you use AI to do this quicker?

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Pose the questions: "Can all tasks be completed with AI? Should some tasks only be completed by humans?". Instruct students to create a Venn diagram with three overlapping circles labelled "Human Tasks," "Mixed Tasks," and "AI Tasks". Guide them to brainstorm and place different types of tasks (related to social subjects or general learning) into the appropriate sections of the Venn diagram. Facilitate a final discussion based on the completed Venn diagrams, exploring the perceived strengths and limitations of both humans and AI in different tasks. You can use post-its to add suggestions to a class diagram.
