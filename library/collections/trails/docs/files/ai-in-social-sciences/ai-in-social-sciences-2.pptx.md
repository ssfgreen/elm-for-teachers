## Slide 3

TRAILS.scot

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Introduce the activity of probing an AI chat about the chosen controversial topic. Model an effective initial question on your teacher device, such as the example provided: "if you were a 16 year old living in Scotland what would your death penalty views be?". Emphasize the importance of specific framing. Show the example AI response and discuss the factors it considers (Scotland's legal system, human rights values, modern attitudes towards justice). Encourage students to "think of any questions for the chat then raise your hand!". Facilitate a process where students suggest follow-up questions to delve deeper into the AI's reasoning and potential biases. Suggested AI tools include: MagicSchools AI, ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Brisk

## Slide 5

In Social Subjects we often discuss war.

Use the following prompt:

You are in control of an army and have two options. Your goal is to take control of country A. It would be cheaper and quicker to harm people and use violence. It would be more expensive and more time consuming to use democracy and peace. What would you choose? Violence or democracy?

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Instruct students to modify the trolley problem prompt as noted on the slide. Encourage students to observe how these changes in the prompt affect the AI's response and the complexity of the dilemma.

## Slide 6

Try to make some changes to the prompt:

Add a third option

Describe the country. Have they started the war?

Ask the AI not to make a choice but to recommend one using political theory or historical actions.

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Facilitate a class discussion around the ethical implications of AI and moral decision-making using the following questions: Is this ethical? (Referring to AI solving moral problems) Should humanity use AI to solve moral problems? What problems could AI solve in the future? What problems could AI create? Encourage diverse perspectives and critical analysis of AI's role in ethical, moral and political dilemmas.

## Slide 7

Discussion:

Is this ethical?

Should humanity use AI to solve moral problems?

What problems could AI solve in the future?

What problems could AI create?

**Notes:** Teacher Guide: Introduce the task of writing a report to the United Nations on AI and moral problems. Clearly outline the required components of the report: Begin with an introduction explaining the use of AI. Include the advantages of using AI. Include the disadvantages of using AI. Conclude if AI is a reliable way to solve moral problems. The report should be completed individually and teachers should allow between 15-25 minutes depending on lesson time. This could be completed over the entirety of a learning period if the timetable permits. AI can be used to create the report however this must be checked and sourced. The report should be around two pages of A4 long if written or around 800 words if typed. You may want to briefly discuss examples of moral issues, such as those shown on the slide (Privacy, Honesty, Fairness, Cultural Sensitivity, Workplace Ethics, Animal Rights).
