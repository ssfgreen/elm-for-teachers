## How AI creates content (Generative AI) – Lesson Plan and Teachers’ Guide

## Introduction

This document provides guidance and supporting materials for teachers delivering the RAI Lesson on Generative AI. It is designed to complement previous lessons in the RAI set. The lesson plan is organized to correspond directly with the accompanying slides, which include a mix of explanatory content, interactive activities, and discussion prompts.

Teachers are encouraged to adapt and modify the lesson as needed—choosing activities that best suit their learners’ needs in a flexible, pick-and-mix style.

## Subject Background

Generative AI is a branch of artificial intelligence that goes beyond analysing or classifying existing data—it creates new content. Building on the foundation established in earlier AI lessons, this session explores how AI models, such as large language models (LLMs) and image generation systems, are designed to generate text, images, and other media based on learned patterns from large datasets.

Key points include:

What It Is: Unlike traditional AI systems that are primarily used for prediction or classification, generative AI models produce original content by mimicking the underlying structures in data.

Core Technologies: The lesson will briefly and simply discuss the technologies behind generative AI and provide examples like GPT for text generation and DALL-E or Stable Diffusion for image creation.

Applications and Implications: While generative AI opens up exciting creative possibilities, it also raises important questions regarding ethics, bias, and the reliability of AI-generated outputs. This lesson will address these opportunities and challenges, preparing students to critically evaluate the role of AI in modern society.

## Lesson Structure

The lesson begins with an overview to what Generative AI is and how it works. Then it goes on to explore text generation and image generation in more detail. At the end of the lesson there is some time to think about the potential problems with Generative AI.

Generative AI – 5 minutes

Text Generation – 20 minutes

Text Generation Explanation and Video – 5 minutes

Let’s generate a sentence Activity OR Let’s write a story activity – 15 minutes

Image Generation – 20 minutes

Prompt Battle – 5 minutes

How does image generation work – 5 minutes

Blorps and Glixers Activity and Discussion Questions – 10 minutes

Potential Problems – 5 minutes

Students consider potential problems of Gen AI and discuss – 5 minutes

## Learning Objectives

Define Generative AI and list some common examples.

Describe simply how AI can generate text using large language models.

Explain simply how AI creates images.

Show how different prompts can change the AI’s output.

Discuss a few challenges and concerns about using Generative AI.

## Text Generation with Large Language Models

This section introduces the concept of Large Language Models through slides and a video.

It is recommended to select either the “Let’s generate a sentence activity”, or “Let’s write a story activity”, depending on the needs of the learners and your preference.

### Activity: Let’s generate a sentence

This activity aims to get students to understand talk generation through carrying out a similar process that real life LLMs use.

Wooclap is a suitable online tool for this exercise. Ask students to write down an animal, a past tense verb and a preposition, all on different word clouds. The largest of each type of word can be selected. However, for this method to work, you will need to have access to Wooclap premium. If you do not have access to Wooclap, this activity can be given with post-it notes or some other method.

There is some preparation for this activity if you would like to use Wooclap that should take no longer than 10 minutes. The steps are as follows:

Create an account or log in to Wooclap

Click “Create Event” and name it something like “Generate a Sentence” or “Generative AI”

Create questions of the Word Cloud type asking for an animal, a past tense verb, a preposition, an object

Share the link with your class and let them give examples of these words live in the classroom

Once the words have been gathered, read out (or ask your class to) the sentence that has been formed:

The [animal] [past tense verb] [preposition] the [object].

#### Details of how this links to generative AI

This activity mimics how large language models (LLMs) generate text. Like students selecting words, LLMs predict the most likely next word based on patterns in their training data.

Pattern Recognition – AI learns from vast datasets, just as students recognize patterns in words.

Randomness & Creativity – AI selects from multiple possibilities, leading to unexpected or humorous results.

Limitations & Bias – Limited input affects output, just as AI models reflect biases in their training data. Our simple model doesn’t connect the words at all – so we might have an elephant flying or a sloth running. This can lead to amusing responses, but we can probably do better!

This hands-on approach helps students grasp how AI generates language and why results vary.

### Activity: Let’s write a story

In this activity, students will collaboratively write a story, simulating how a large language model (LLM) generates text with limited context. Hopefully we can do better than our ‘Generate a sentence’ baby chat-gpt model!

The learners will carry out the following steps:

Steps:

Each student writes a word and passes the paper on.

The next student adds a word below it and passes it on.

This continues until the fourth word is written. Then, the first word is folded over to hide it.

With each new word added, the oldest visible word is folded away, ensuring only three words are visible at a time.

Key Learning Point:

Limited Context: Like LLMs, students can only see a small portion of previous words, mimicking how AI generates text based on limited memory rather than full context.

Emergent Meaning: The story may take unexpected turns, just as AI outputs can sometimes be unpredictable or surprising.

Encourage students to reflect on how this process affects coherence and creativity, linking it back to AI text generation.

## Image Generation

### Activity: Prompt Battle

In this activity, students compete to generate the best AI-generated image, learning about effective prompt writing and how inputs shape outputs. They will also explore the strengths and limitations of AI tools.

#### Setup & Tools

If available, students can use AI tools like Microsoft Create, Deep AI, or DALL·E on their devices.

If only accessible on a main class computer, images can be generated at the front of the room.

#### Steps

Form small groups and have each group create a detailed AI image prompt.

Generate the images using an AI tool (either on personal devices or as a class).

Compare and vote – students discuss which image best matches its prompt and vote on their favourite.

#### Key Learning Points

Prompt Specificity: How small changes in wording impact results.

Bias & Limitations: Noticing any unexpected patterns or problems in AI outputs.

Creativity & Experimentation: Encouraging iteration to improve prompts.

This hands-on challenge helps students understand prompt engineering and how AI interprets language to create images.

#### Staying legal and safe

Please ensure you follow your local authority’s guidance on use of GenAI with learners, and if you are asking learners to use the tools, only use AI tools suitable for users under the age of 18.

If this is a challenge, one alternative approach would be to have the learners (in teams or individually) plan out and type up a prompt and send it to the teacher to then run the AI query.

This will also mean the browser window is under the control of an adult and can be quickly closed on the unlikely chance inappropriate content is displayed by the AI system.

### Blorps and Glixers

This activity helps students understand how AI generates new content based on learned patterns. Students will use their knowledge from the Classification lesson to create their own Blorp or Glixer, just as an AI model generates new images based on existing data.

#### Activity Steps:

Recap – Remind students of the characteristics that define Blorps and Glixers.

Create – Students draw a new Blorp or Glixer, following the observed traits.

Compare – Discuss how their creations align with or differ from the original examples.

Explain that just as AI learns from existing data, students will use their previous knowledge from the Classification lesson to generate new content.

#### Discussion Prompts & Suggested Answers:

How does this activity mirror the creative process of AI? AI does not “imagine” like humans; it generates content by recognizing patterns from its training data, just as students create their drawings based on previous examples.

What might be some limitations if the AI only learned from a narrow dataset? The AI might struggle to generate diverse outputs. If trained only on one type of Blorp, it may fail to recognize or create variations, leading to bias or repetitive results.

## Potential Problems

At the end of the lesson, students will reflect on the challenges and ethical concerns surrounding generative AI. Encouraging students to think about these challenges themselves is very beneficial as it helps them develop critical thinking skills, encourages ethical awareness, and prepares them to engage responsibly with AI technologies in the future.

### Key Issues to Highlight:

Bias in AI Outputs – AI models reflect biases in their training data, leading to skewed or unfair results.

Misinformation & Deepfakes – AI-generated content can be used to spread false information.

Creativity & Originality – AI can assist in creative work, but does it replace human creativity?

Ethical & Copyright Concerns – AI models may generate content that closely resembles copyrighted material.

### Discussion Prompts & Suggested Insights

What are some benefits and drawbacks of using Generative AI in creative fields? Benefits: Faster content creation, idea generation, accessibility for non-artists. Drawbacks: Ethical concerns, job displacement, potential loss of originality.

How might biases in training data affect the output of a generative model? If an AI is trained only on Western art, it may struggle to generate diverse styles. Biases in datasets can reinforce stereotypes or exclude certain perspectives.
