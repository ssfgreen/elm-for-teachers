## Contents

One collection, pedagogy-scotland, for P7 Literacy and English and S1 history. It holds 116 entries: 15 principles, 13 activity types, 10 subject pedagogies, 23 adaptations, 10 format rules, 12 cautions, 30 curriculum links and 3 exemplars. Each entry is split into claims (437 in all), and each claim is backed by passages (377) from 115 named sources. Passages are paraphrases, or quotes of 15 words or fewer. Most entries carry an evidence rating, from practitioner wisdom to strong. Four rate a practice as not recommended (three) or contested (one).

Every entry is a draft that no teacher or specialist has reviewed. Each source keeps its own licence, and most are not yet confirmed, so the plugin is for use only, not for adapting.

_Drafted by an assistant. Not yet approved by an author._

## Expected use

For teachers planning and adapting worksheets in Elm for Schools, in class and year group chats. The assistant looks entries up with the tools entries, entry, claims and facets, grounds its choices in them, and cites each claim it uses, so the teacher can see where a suggestion comes from. A teacher can hide entries, favour entries, and ask for entries rated at least a given level of evidence.

It supports the teacher's own judgement about their class; it does not replace it.

## Risks

Every entry is a draft: an entry or a claim may be wrong, out of date or rated too high, and no teacher or specialist has checked them. The curriculum links follow the Curriculum for Excellence experiences and outcomes and Benchmarks, which are under review, so a link may go out of date. The entries cover P7 Literacy and English and S1 history, mostly from Scottish sources; outside those the assistant may stretch an entry too far.

The teacher decides which adaptations suit a pupil, planning from the pupil's needs rather than a label. It is not for assessment, or for decisions about a pupil's additional support, which follow the school's own process. Some sources' licences are not confirmed, so passages must not be copied into other materials.
